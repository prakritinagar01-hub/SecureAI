CYBERSECURITY AWARENESS

How to avoid phishing.

Phishing tricks you into sharing passwords, OTPs or money. These habits protect you even when a message looks completely real.

Red flags to watch for

If a message shows even one of these, stop and verify.

Urgency and threats

"Your account will be blocked in 24 hours." Scammers rush you so you do not think.

Requests for OTP or PIN

No genuine bank, company or government office will ask for your OTP, PIN or password.

Prizes you never entered

Lottery wins, free gifts and "claim your reward" messages are almost always scams.

Strange links

Shortened links, IP addresses, extra words like "secure-login" or an @ inside the address.

Payment before a reward

Being asked to pay a "processing fee" to receive money or a prize is a classic trick.

Unknown sender, personal tone

Unexpected messages from unknown numbers, or from a "friend" asking for urgent money.

Good habits

Small routines that make you much harder to scam.

Before you click

  • Do not tap links in unexpected SMS, email or WhatsApp messages.
  • Type the website address yourself or use the official app.
  • Check that the address starts with https and the domain is spelled correctly.
  • Scan the link here if you are unsure.

Protect your accounts

  • Use a different strong password for every important account.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered.
  • Use a password manager instead of reusing passwords.
  • Keep your phone, apps and browser updated.

Handle calls and chats

  • Hang up on callers who ask for OTPs, card numbers or remote access.
  • Never install screen-sharing apps because a stranger asked you to.
  • Call your bank using the number printed on your card or official website.
  • Talk to family before sending money after an "emergency" message.

Payments and UPI

  • You never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money.
  • Be careful with QR codes: scanning a QR code is for paying, not receiving.
  • Check the payee name before you confirm any payment.
  • Be wary of fake customer-care numbers found through search results.

If you already clicked or shared something

Act quickly. The first hour matters most.

  1. Stop engaging. Do not reply, call back or click anything else.
  2. Change your passwords for the affected account and any account that reused it.
  3. Call your bank immediately if you shared card, OTP or UPI details, and ask them to block the card or account.
  4. Turn on two-factor authentication and check recent login activity.
  5. Report it. In India, call the National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 or file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.
